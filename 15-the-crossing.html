Aire valley, Ardennes · 3–9 October 1918

The crossing at La Forge

Eight days of ground, taken from the regiment’s own war diary and set on the modern valley. Step through the days; the lines say how firmly each one is known.

The 110th Infantry spent the first week of October 1918 on the wrong side of the Aire. It had come up the east bank from Apremont; the Germans held the hills on the west. On the night of 6–7 October the regiment crossed the river in the dark at La Forge and attacked Châtel-Chéhéry at half past five in the morning, with the 3rd Battalion leading — and Company L in the 3rd Battalion.

Almost nothing on this map is a surveyed point. What the records give are named places, compass directions and distances in kilometres, and the map is drawn to show exactly how much of it is testimony and how much is reasoning. Solid lines are stated in the sources. Dashed lines are worked out from a neighbouring unit’s account. Dotted lines are argument. Where the honest answer is an area rather than a line — the crossing, the division boundary — it is drawn as a band.

      The grid in the margin The tick numbers along the edges are the wartime survey grid — Lambert Nord de Guerre, the same grid printed on the 1918 sheets and on the graves registration plat. A coordinate written in the regimental records as Co-ord. 00.2 — 77.9 is E 300.2 / N 277.9 on these margins. The fit used here reproduces the coordinate written on Plat A-122 in 1919 to within fourteen metres of the grave it names, which is as close as a hand-plotted wartime coordinate gets.
