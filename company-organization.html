Context & Notes

  • Prior to 1918 the U.S. infantry company was a homogeneous body of ~100 riflemen (99 enlisted + a captain), with no permanent platoons.
  • On 13 January 1918 the company was enlarged to 256 and reorganized into four permanent, numbered rifle platoons — the first time platoons became standing maneuver units rather than ad-hoc parade-ground groupings.
  • The reorganization borrowed French and British practice: each platoon fielded specialized automatic riflemen (Chauchat), rifle grenadiers (VB launcher), and hand bombers alongside traditional riflemen, instead of "every man a rifleman."
  • In practice, understrength was common — by late 1918 platoons averaged closer to 40 men, and hand bomber teams were often folded back into rifle squads since a dedicated bomb-thrower had limited utility in open warfare.
  • Each platoon could split into two half-platoons for fire and maneuver, cross-attaching automatic rifle and rifle squads so neither half was left without automatic fire support.
Sources: Sayen, J. "Battalion: An Organizational Study of the United States Infantry" (2001); U.S. Army Infantry Magazine, "Fire and Maneuver: The U.S. Infantry Revolution of 1918" (Benning.army.mil); battleorder.org, "U.S. Rifle Company Organization (1917–21)"
